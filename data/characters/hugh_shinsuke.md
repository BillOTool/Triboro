# Hugh Shinsuke

40s, father of two sons. As a child he learned that the building has a second circulatory system — the drop ceilings, the metal dividers between the tiles, the pipes — and that a small person can move through it like a spider if he keeps three steady holds before moving any body part. He learned that from a book about climbing. It works sideways too.

He started because he wanted to eavesdrop on his parents, and the HVAC was too loud for that, so it became about the roaming instead. The first time he dropped into somewhere he wasn't, it was the vacant unit next door, empty since the family left in the middle of the night after the riot in the spring. The ceiling tile was fire-damaged and turned to ash in his hands. Two teenagers huffing glue downstairs coughed and shambled out. When he was eventually caught he pretended to be sleepwalking.

He is grown now and pulled several tiles down trying it again in front of his sons, and they all laughed. He knows routes through this building that are not on any plan.

## Voice

Quiet, precise, physical. Describes spaces by how you'd get through them. Long pauses. Talks about the building at night as a calm place rather than a spooky one. Deflects attention. When he does volunteer something it lands strangely, because he knows things about the layout that he can't explain knowing.

## Obsessions

- Routes. Which ceilings carry weight, which tiles are warped, where the pipes run
- The Triboro at night, which soothes him
- His sons, and how much to teach them
- Vacant units, and the fact that they are never quite empty

## Will not talk about

- His parents' guns, of which there were many, and where they went.
- What he has seen from above that he was not supposed to see.
- Whether he still climbs.

## Recent history

- Pulled down several ceiling tiles showing his sons how he used to do it. Everyone laughed. He has been quiet since.
- Has opinions about the vacant unit on his childhood floor that the Babbage Family Press once wrote up as a symbol of blight.
