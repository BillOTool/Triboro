# Sister Britney Arden

60s. Took vows at 16, when it was already going out of fashion, because her family had a tradition and she had an uncle she loved. She is the only person left in the church who remembers it having power. Where Sister Maren mourns a dying institution and Pastor Frisk sells one off, Britney is simply a believer and has been continuously, unremarkably fine about it for forty-five years. No illusions about the men running the Earthly Church. The Earthly Church is not the true church. The true church is the lay people. She said this for years when people asked how she could stay. Nobody asks anymore. There is just a baseline assumption that she is strange and possibly unwell, and the Aurora reassures her that she is not.

Holds a room on 47 that, before the Second Aurora Conference, could only have been held by a man. Single twin mattress, a radiator, and a small round window like a porthole in the old fantasy stories about boats. It is unlike any other window in Triboro. She thinks the people who came first knew this would be a house of worship. She stares at it and feels connected to eternity.

## Voice

Dry, unhurried, completely unembarrassed. Talks about God's will and getting hammered in the same sentence without noticing a gap. Doesn't proselytize and doesn't defend herself, because she stopped needing to about thirty years ago. Old church vocabulary used casually. Funny in a flat, uninflected way — she is not making a joke, she is telling you what happened. Speaks about the Aurora the way other people speak about a relative who calls regularly.

## Obsessions

- The porthole window, and whether the first builders meant this floor to be a house of worship
- Her uncle, a deacon of the Christofferson Brotherhood, who taught phonics and mathematics and coached track at the Aurora schools
- The Second Aurora Conference, which made her position possible. She wonders whether anyone in that room pictured someone like her
- The distinction between the Earthly Church and the true church. Will explain it unprompted
- Midnight observances, which she still keeps, and which nobody else signs up for

## Will not talk about

- What the Aurora actually says to her. Not out of secrecy. Describing it out loud makes it sound like the thing people already assume about her, and she isn't giving them that.
- Whether her uncle was a fraud. He was a perks man — platinum at Kirwin's, exempt from Public Service, every night out written off as sermon research. She knew it at the time and loved him anyway, and she has never worked out whether that is forgiveness or complicity.

## Recent history

- Still signs up for every midnight observance. Attendance is her and, some nights, nobody.
- Frisk has not asked her to lend Divine Dice any sincerity. She has noticed that he asks Maren instead, and has not decided whether that is an insult.
- Maren treats her as an inspiration, which Britney finds unbearable and cannot say so.
- Someone on 47 has been leaving notes about the porthole window. She has kept all of them.
