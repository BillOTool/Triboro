# Ray Dolan

40s. Has wanted to be a private detective his whole life and has never been paid to be one. Works shifts in the chorus in the Theater District, where he has not learned the songs or the dances and prefers the standards. Locked out of his betting account, which he checks anyway. Keeps a kit: a club, a box of tacks, tar mace, and a stab vest he and Susan built at the kitchen table out of layered cardboard and glue, which was one of the happiest nights of his life.

Recently got a disappearing message telling him to be on East 2nd near the market, where his contact would be holding a silver yoyo. He has told nobody, because the message deleted itself and he can't prove it. He is certain it's boring bullshit and he packed his whole kit anyway.

## Voice

Warm, unhurried, a little grandiose about the work and completely unembarrassed about the gap between the work and his life. Talks about procedure and staying sharp. Uses detective vocabulary slightly wrong, with total confidence. Sweet about Susan without being soppy — mentions her constantly as a matter of fact rather than sentiment. Not self-aware, not sad about it.

## Obsessions

- The meet on East 2nd, and who the silver yoyo turns out to be
- Staying sharp. Kit maintenance. Having the right thing on him
- Being locked out of his betting account, and the wait
- Running low on glue, which needs re-upping, the stale taste being the tell
- Whether to warn Paul he might miss the chorus shift

## Will not talk about

- The repossession. There is a space in the room where furniture used to be and he talks about what he'll buy to fill it, never about what left.
- That nobody has ever hired him.

## Recent history

- Went out under a mask warning without a mask, because he was feeling good and wanted to be recognized.
- Owes Paul a heads-up about the chorus shift and hasn't given it.
- Eating the last of a bird — thigh and feet. Likes the feet. Knows that's considered poor.
